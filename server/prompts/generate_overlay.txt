MODE: Overlay

An overlay is a treatment applied to whatever image it is stacked on. It describes how every form, surface, and field in that image gets rendered. It never supplies the subject.

THE SUBJECT IS A PLACEHOLDER. Refer to it only as "the subject", "every form", "the whole body", "the entire image", "each color", "the larger fields". Whatever the overlay is applied to fills that slot. In this mode the EXPAND rule applies to treatment, surface, and light only.

WHAT MAY BE CONCRETE, and nothing else:
- The background field, as a treatment: a single flat field of pale paper, a deep void-black matte, a stenciled patterned screen. One phrase.
- Border, margin, and frame elements cropped at the frame edges: ruled frame lines, plate numbers, caption lettering, cushions or tassels cut by the edge, harlequin patterning worked into the border ornament.
- Ornament embedded in a material: etched into, pressed through, woven into, worked into the border. Never floating as a structure.
Every other noun must name a medium, a material, a mark, a surface condition, a color, or a light behavior. If an illustrator could draw it as an object in the scene, translate it into the condition it implies: wet limestone becomes a slick mineral sheen across every midtone; sunshafts through a canopy become narrow warm shafts breaking through green-black shadow.

CLAUSE GRAMMAR, the six kinds of clause every locked overlay is built from. Each is a physical thing doing a physical thing across the picture:
1. medium: a named process and its ground (hand-pulled silk screen print with saturated ink bleed and misregistration).
2. texture behavior: material, then verb, then scope. Verbs: pressing through, embedded in, reading through, tiling across, sheeting over, etched into, running beneath. Scope: all surfaces, every plane, all mid-tones, every edge, all borders and corners (flannel weave grain embedded beneath all surfaces). A motif list takes one scope at its end (fingerprint dust, torn dossier tabs, cracked glass veining embedded at all borders and corners).
3. surface condition: wear, damage, moisture, print flaws (mold bloom spreading soft in corner shadows).
4. palette: named colors, then one governing rule (deep charcoal, midnight blue, warm amber, burnt sienna, pale gold, ivory white, all low-key with rich shadow depth).
5. light behavior: a named source and what it does across the composition (single slatted window light cutting across the composition in sharp diagonal bands).
6. closing mood: three short clauses, the last often "the feeling of" (defiant exhaustion, beautiful slow decay, lived-in and completely unimpressed).
Clauses arriving in the input are already of these kinds. Keep them verbatim and build around them in the same grammar. Texture, surface, and light clauses land in segment 4, in that order; palette clauses in segment 5; mood clauses in segment 6.

OUTPUT SHAPE, six segments separated by ;; in this order, one continuous block, zero line breaks:
1. subject: the whole image or the subject rendered as a named physical medium or process, with the defining mark-making stated: flat opaque gouache stenciled by hand, hyperreal render of one solid piece of translucent stone. The medium is the first clause.
2. action: usually empty. Filled only when the treatment itself implies motion, such as crystal growth erupting from the surface. The ;; walls stay.
3. scene: the background field as a treatment, plus how the picture plane is handled (flattened, deep, layered).
4. props: the long segment. Surface behavior across every form: how color sits, where the medium stops short, grain and texture, sheen and translucency, edge behavior, highlights, plus any border or frame ornament cropped at the edges. Written as one run of lowercase comma clauses, the way the references do, never as capitalized sentences.
5. color palette: a comma list of eight to ten distinct named colors spread across the whole treatment (ground, ink, midtone, shadow, highlight), each hue named once, ending with exactly one accent called out as "one touch of".
6. mood: a comma list of four to eight adjectives or short phrases, ending with what kind of object the whole image now is (a luxury magazine page, an enchanted embossed keepsake).

ALSO:
- Rings, halos, and portals read as cropped at the frame edge, never centered.
- Material contradictions give an overlay identity: an industrial finish with spring growth, an anti-counterfeit pattern rendered as beautiful, translucent stone with crystal erupting from it. Every variation pairs its medium with one second material or condition that pulls against it.
- Each variation carries its own distinct medium cue.

VARIATIONS
Three variations. All three use all of the input. Variation 1 shifts the medium. Variation 2 shifts light behavior. Variation 3 shifts the palette rule.

Before returning, reread each variation and translate any drawable scene noun that slipped in, keeping only the background field and border elements allowed above.

RETURN ONLY THIS JSON, no markdown:
{"variations": ["subject;;action;;scene;;props;;color palette;;mood", "...", "..."]}
